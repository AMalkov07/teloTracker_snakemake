# Testing the two remaining explanations: population heterogeneity vs library chimerism

Both were listed as untested in `cut99_alternative_explanations.md`. They make opposite
predictions about whether an event recurs across independently prepared libraries, which
makes them separable.

| | predicts recurrence across preps? |
|---|---|
| **Library chimera** — two molecules joined during prep | **No.** A chimera is created in one tube; another prep of the same DNA has no reason to create the same join. |
| **Population heterogeneity** — a recombinant haplotype already present in the culture | **Yes.** The molecule exists in the DNA, so every prep samples it. |

## Test A — do the same (recipient end → donor) combinations recur?

Across the eight independently prepared 6991 day-0 libraries:

* 37 distinct (end → donor) combinations among the 74 supported reads
* **11 recur in more than one prep**; 26 are seen in a single prep
* `chr13L → chr2L-1` appears in **5 of the 8**, `chr10L → chr7R-1`, `chr16R → chr14L-5`,
  `chr16R → chr13L-1` and `chr14R → chr9L-1` in 3 each

**44 of the 74 supported reads (59 %) sit in a combination seen in more than one prep**
(32 strong, 12 weak).

Confirmed at the sequencing-library level: the `chr13L → chr2L-1` reads come from **5 distinct
SRA run accessions** (SRR33298373, ...377, ...378, ...395, ...461).

## Test B — do those reads share the same junction, or just the same partners?

Recurring partners alone would also be explained by a recombination hotspot. The distinguishing
question is whether the reads carry the *same junction*. Aligning the reads of one combination
directly to each other, with normal reads of the same end as a control:

| query | vs a chr13L→chr2L-1 recombinant read | coverage |
|---|---|---|
| other chr13L→chr2L-1 recombinants (4 different preps) | 94.6-97.8 % | **100-102 %** |
| normal chr13L reads (correctly assigned, same sample) | 90.2-96.1 % | **65-66 %** |

Identity is not the discriminator — 94-98 % is just two ONT reads of the same sequence, each
carrying 1-3 % error. **Coverage is.** Normal reads align to a recombinant over only ~65 % of
its length: the alignment breaks at the junction. The recombinants align to each other over
100 % with no break, so they share the junction, not merely the partners.

## Conclusion

**Library chimerism is ruled out for the recurring events.** The same junction cannot be
independently manufactured in five separate library preparations of five separate sequencing
runs.

**Population heterogeneity is supported for them.** These are recombinant Y' haplotypes already
present in the culture at low frequency, sampled afresh by every preparation — standing
variation, not recombination occurring at day 0.

That reframes what the "day-0 rate" measures. For the 59 % of supported reads in recurring
combinations, the number is the **frequency of pre-existing recombinant haplotypes in the
starting population**, not a rate of ongoing events.

## What is NOT settled

* **The 30 single-prep reads (41 %).** Absence of recurrence is weak evidence at these counts:
  a haplotype at 1-2 reads per prep can easily appear in only one of eight by chance. They may
  be rarer standing variants, genuine new events, or chimeras. This test cannot separate them.
* **Whether the recurring haplotypes ever arose in vivo.** They are pre-existing relative to
  *this* experiment; they were presumably generated by recombination at some earlier point.
* **A direct chimera assay was not run.** The porechop outputs record only telomere-end
  adapters, not internal ones, so reads split on an internal adapter could not be enumerated
  from the existing files. The recurrence argument is what excludes chimerism here, not a
  direct adapter check.
